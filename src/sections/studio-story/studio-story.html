The argument

02 / 05

Why it is all one room.

Most residential projects fail in the gaps between consultants. The architect draws a wall, the engineer sizes a beam that will not fit inside it, and the render shows a window that the framing plan never had room for. Nobody is wrong. The information simply never met.

Three disciplines in one room is not a convenience. It is the only arrangement in which an argument can be settled while it is still cheap to settle. The engineer sizes a ridge beam while the ceiling above it is still a question; the visualiser lights a room and notices that the clerestory has nowhere to sit. Each of those is a ten-minute conversation across a desk, and a three-week exchange of PDFs between offices.

We removed the gaps. One coordinated model produces the permit set, the site plan and the renders, so a change in any of them is a change in all of them. It is slower for the first two weeks and considerably faster for the next six months.

BIM-first is a technical phrase for a plain commercial fact: your drawings cannot quietly disagree with one another. Move a window and the elevation, the schedule, the header above it and the render all move in the same afternoon. The change orders that arrive on site are almost never about design — they are about sheets that stopped matching, and that is the thing this removes.

The practice

03 / 05

From the first sheet
to this morning.

Six things changed the way this office draws. The rest — the software, the office, the number of desks — followed from them.

  1. 2009

    The first sheet

    The practice opens in one room off East Sixth with a licensed architect, a plotter and a rule that nothing gets promised until it has been drawn. By 2012 there is a structural engineer at the next desk.

  2. 2015

    The civil desk

    Site plans stop being somebody else’s problem. Grading, drainage and impervious cover move in house, and the permit calendar loses whole weeks of waiting.

  3. 2018

    Renders off the model

    Visualisation comes in house on one condition: no material appears in an image that is not specified in the set. The renders become evidence rather than marketing.

  4. 2021

    One sheet standard

    We write the modelling and sheet-numbering standard the whole studio now argues about. Schedules generate from the model, so a beam cannot be one size in plan and another in section.

  5. 2023

    Three hundred sets

    The three-hundredth permit-ready set leaves the office in the spring. The four-hundredth takes less than two years to follow it.

  6. Today

    Still one room

    Three disciplines, one model, and a first-submittal approval rate we publish rather than claim. The next set starts on Monday.
